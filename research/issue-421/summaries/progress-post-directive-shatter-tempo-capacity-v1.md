# Issue #421 progress — Shatter-to-heavy-Attack capacity closure

## Decision

The preregistered zero-row screen reached decision boundary 2: **close the
Shatter-to-heavy-Attack tempo relay without implementation**. It did not
authorise a research knob, a simulator row, content scope or held-out work.

This is a bounded negative. It says that the exact frozen consumer class is too
broad to support the missing Duskblade repertoire split; it does not say that
heavy Attacks or Shatter are ineffective.

## Frozen mechanism and cohort

The proposed relay would have armed one non-stacking charge after a real
Duskblade Shatter. The next reward-pool Attack with printed cost at least 2
would have consumed that charge only when it saved exactly 1 Energy. It could
not change damage, Ward, draw, healing, chip, offers, rarity, acquisition or
policy scores, and could not fire for Ashwarden.

The deterministic source filter selected all six and only the unlocked
current-main reward Attacks meeting that definition: Annihilate, Quarry Maul,
Thirsting Shard, Bellstrike, Hailglass and Ringing Blow. No card was singled out
after counts were observed.

Capacity was read from the frozen root-551 Duskblade Vow-5 rectangle: 64 policy
identities, four simulation seeds each and 256 existing rows. A robust active
policy required Shatter and an eligible played Attack in at least two rows; a
robust inactive policy required no such co-occurrence. Chisel plus Faultline
plays supplied only a necessary non-alias proxy for the already admitted exact
Scoreline package.

## Result

- all 64 policies were robustly Shatter-ready;
- 53 were robustly relay-active, 10 ambiguous and only 1 robustly inactive,
  against the frozen minimum of 16 inactive policies;
- all 64 were offered an eligible Attack and 63 acquired one;
- all six eligible cards were consumed by robustly active policies;
- 17 policies were relay-only under the Scoreline capacity proxy, with Jaccard
  0.632, so the non-alias gate passed;
- no active policy won at least two of its four frozen Vow-5 rows, so the
  deliberately strict capacity-level viability proxy also failed;
- one pre-existing baseline fault row was observed; no intervention existed and
  therefore no fault can be attributed to the proposed relay.

The broadness failure is decisive by itself. Subsetting cards, relaxing the
inactive-policy minimum or redefining viability after seeing these counts is
forbidden. The next structural mechanism must have a narrower state or action
consumer before implementation.
