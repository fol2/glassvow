## 1. Concrete law and player decision

All new names below are PROPOSALS, not IDs claimed to exist in M. Values are the declaration's only values.

| Role | Proposed card | Authored cost/type/target | Base | Upgraded |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Producer P | `setTheAngle` / Set the Angle | 1 Energy, uncommon Skill, one living enemy | Gain 4 Block, anchor that enemy | Gain 6 Block, same anchor law |
| Consumer C | `crosscut` / Crosscut | 1 Energy, uncommon Attack, one living enemy | Hit selected B for 5; conditionally hit anchored A for 5 | Both hits 7; same enabling/reset law |

The producer's ordinary Block uses existing Dex/Frail rules. The consumer's two possible hits are ordinary Attack hits, not raw damage; native Strength, Weak, Cracked, Seal, Block, Thorns and death apply. Producer and consumer upgrades are independent. No parameter was chosen from a game outcome.

The visible mediator is **one nullable reference to the identical living enemy object in the current CombatState**. A public marker identifies it. This is neither a card UID nor an enemy type nor an inferred intent. It does not change any enemy stat merely by being installed.

**P transition.** Validate the actual hand instance, current effective cost, legal target and live combat through the ordinary command path. Pay and perform ordinary Block. If Duskblade, record that selected living enemy as the sole anchor; replacing an existing anchor never stacks. P adds no direct damage, chip, draw, Energy, Ember or Strength. Later native death clears/inactivates an anchor normally.

**C transition.** Validate and pay the independently playable Attack even when no anchor exists. Capture and clear the anchor at the start of its legal effect. Resolve the ordinary primary hit on B. Only if the player survives, combat has not terminated or handed off, the captured A is the identical living member of this CombatState, A differs from B, and the aspect is Duskblade, resolve exactly one ordinary return hit on A. An invalid, missing or same-target anchor is consumed without a return, not retained for a later C. Return resolution never recursively invokes C, re-arms the anchor or counts as a second card/Attack command.

**Settlement.** Both ordinary hits precede the existing single deferred chip settlement for the card. Each connected living target participates according to the existing per-card/per-target rule, including Beacon and the full coupled Shatter/Stun/Cracked consequences. Do not run settlement after the primary and then again after the return. Existing post-card effects run once. Because C remains authored `target=enemy`, existing Venomous is directed at selected B, NOT both A and B as if the card were `allEnemies`. Other collateral remains native; the package is not isolated by suppressing it.

**Expiry and identity.** Clear before the enemy phase, on terminal/handoff/abandon, on constructing/reconstructing combat, and on anchor death. Membership plus object identity is required after the first hit. Reusing an index, name or type in another fight cannot inherit an anchor. No saved, cross-turn, cross-fight or card-copy continuation exists. Any number of legal unrelated commands may intervene during the same player turn. Only P installs this state and only the named C consumes it; generic attacks, Honing, Flurry, Echo and an observer do not acquire implicit hooks.

**Player trade-off.** The player spends a card and Energy, accepts less standalone Block, selects which enemy should receive the eventual return, and gives up immediate focus-fire/stronger defence/another play. C is independently legal but a weaker standalone Attack; a useful return needs both affordable roles and at least two living enemies at the right time. The player may deliberately mark a low-Block non-primary enemy, preserve it until C, or decline the package when killing B, blocking damage or concentrating damage is better. Public HP, Block, intents, Energy, hand and the marker suffice. No hidden order, seed, future draw or actor label is needed.

This is not claimed to be attractive at the declared numbers. M's ordinary Strike is 6/9 and Defend 5/8 at cost 1; those are opportunity-cost comparators, not a balance test. Single-enemy fights give no package payoff. There is no accumulated reward for waiting across turns, but preserving a second enemy could still produce a stalling incentive; native duration/stall hard guards must reject that rather than credit it as strategy.

### Acquisition and source change

Prospectively add the two proposed IDs to the ordinary uncommon pool, with existing ordinary reveal/profile eligibility, never a free starter, granted card, new deed or progression bypass. M's `RewardRules.card_pool` is shared, not a pre-existing Dusk-only pool. A minimal explicit Dusk eligibility predicate for ONLY these two IDs must apply in both the base-pool iteration and the unlocked-card append path. `shop_inventory` consumes the same pool. Preserve existing order for old entries and all current IDs. Do not accidentally permit an unlocked-card path to leak a new card to Ashwarden.

No natural acquisition probability is inferred from a pool entry. Normal offer generation, rejection, purchase cost, hand co-presence and energy remain real. Adding eligible Dusk cards can change Dusk RNG-index-to-offer mappings; deterministic RNG and unchanged draw semantics do NOT mean byte-identical old Dusk offers. No extra RNG call or seed manipulation is part of the new law. Null existing-combat paths and the excluded-aspect pool must be checked separately.

### Bounded structural-cost ranking

Compare implementations of this SAME law, not additional topologies: (1) one CombatState reference plus a marker; (2) per-enemy marks plus a sole-owner arbitration field; (3) persisted run/save identity; (4) reconstructing gameplay state by scanning presentation/observer history. Choice (1) has no new save field, one fight-local field, two card effects and narrow pool/preview/marker seams. (2) needs additional uniqueness/reset work without additional permitted behaviour. (3) adds migration/cross-fight identity obligations forbidden here. (4) makes event consumption/history a gameplay dependency and is rejected. This ranking is only over these encodings; it is not a theorem of global minimum design cost.

## 3. Executed non-native falsification

`crosscut_model.py` is a small standard-library transition projection, not a simulator replacement. `check_crosscut.py` calls its transitions and derives all decisions from resulting states/events. Run from this directory: `python check_crosscut.py`. CHECKS.json is the actual canonical stdout. EXECUTION.json records Python 3.13.5, exit codes, exact tested file hashes, repeat equality and actual mutant invocations. No expected-result flag is fed into the law to cause a PASS.

Result: **40 named checks, 216 finite constructed states, zero failed checks**. The domain is consumer upgrade {base,up}, aspect {Dusk,other}, anchor and selected target each {A,B,C}, anchor Block {0,5,10}, Strength {0,2}. That Cartesian product is 216, not a seed cohort or a sample-size claim. Six deliberately faulty variants were actually invoked and all returned exit 2: copy-name alias, ignored Thorns, same-target return, retained anchor, premature chip settlement, sticky end-turn reset. Repetition was byte-identical; compilation exited 0.

The base positive trace pays two cards/two Energy for 4 Block, one Attack command, primary 5 on B and physical return 5 on A; C remains untouched. Removing P or C, reversing order, using another aspect or expiring the marker removes the new return. Replacement overwrites rather than stacks. A different consumer UID works, but a second C cannot reuse the marker. A stale same-name enemy from another CombatState fails membership.

Component controls retain BOTH legal commands and their native payloads. Cells (producer enabled, consumer enabled) 00, 01, 10 have physical return 0; 11 has 5. All four still have 4 Block and primary damage 5. Thus the sum/union of disabled new factors is not the complete chain, but the whole commands are emphatically NOT zero-valued nulls. The check name containing `proper-subsets` describes these two new factors, not completion of #547's native MEC/proper-subset obligation.

Adverse observations are part of the result, not discarded failures: fully blocked return has zero HP payoff; 5 reported overkill removes only 2 HP from a 2-HP anchor; native-style sequential flooring with modifiers gives 6 rather than the printed 5; lethal primary-hit Thorns and finale handoff suppress the return; nonfinal death carries the Reaper energy/draw obligation. Coupled Shatter sets both Stun and Cracked; Adamant's first hold is not invented as a Shatter. Early settlement would let Bell kill A before return; the declared order returns first. Bell can also remove 4 HP from A with BOTH new factors off, decisively falsifying a naive `anchor lost HP => package enacted` observer.

**Explicit abstraction boundary:** fixtures need not be natively reachable. The projection includes selected native-style arithmetic, connected-target chip ordering, identity, terminal and callback obligations only. It does not implement whole enemy turns, actual draws/shuffles, Prism/Smolder random collateral, every quest/phase/death callback, full card library, offer/pool progression, UI, native saves, policies or end-to-end outcomes. `reaper_draws_owed` is an obligation, not an invented card. The end_turn function checks expiry, not a full next-turn simulation. The alias/observer comparisons are structural proofs within the stated domain, not native causal certificates.

Resource-denied commands return ILLEGAL_UNAVAILABLE without mutating state. Their counterfactual continuation is UNKNOWN, not a free zero-payoff continuation. Effective first-play discounts are retained in a focused control; the model does not replace the complete native cost function. There is no claim that the printed 5/7 bounds realised damage under arbitrary modifiers. Actual physical return HP is at most the anchor's removable HP at that event; other downstream collateral requires separate attribution.
