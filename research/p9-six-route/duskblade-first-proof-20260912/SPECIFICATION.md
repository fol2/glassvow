### Binary Chernoff/KL intervals, with simultaneous coverage

Let `q=k/n`, `m=256`, `alpha542=0.025`, and `c=log(2*m/alpha542)/n`. Define

`d(q,p) = q*log(q/p) + (1-q)*log((1-q)/(1-p))`

with the usual continuous limits at q=0 or q=1. The interval is all `p in [0,1]` for which `d(q,p) <= c`. Compute the lower and upper roots by 70-step bisection; return endpoints outward with a 1e-12 numerical guard. Reject invalid/nonfinite counts; do not replace it with Wald, an uncorrected 95% interval or a post-hoc bootstrap.

Reason for this choice: for a fixed binary finite population, the exponential moment of a simple random sample without replacement is bounded by the corresponding with-replacement exponential moment. Chernoff minimisation therefore gives each one-sided tail bound `exp(-n*d(q,p))`. Each two-sided primitive interval has noncoverage at most `alpha542/256`; the union bound over the predeclared intervals is at most 0.025 without requiring independent metrics. This is a conservative fixed-sample bound, not a time-uniform confidence sequence. It also applies to the fixed-size conditional eligible-root selections described in §5; if their uniform-selection premise is not established, no such inference is claimed.

For paired binary outcomes X,Y, count `g = # (X=1,Y=0)` and `l = # (X=0,Y=1)` over the same root set. Obtain separate primitive intervals G,L. The difference interval is `[G.low-L.high, G.high-L.low]`. It exploits pairing without calling two cloned outcomes independent. For balanced prediction improvement, average the on and off paired-difference intervals; the same simultaneous argument covers the derived comparison.

Pass a lower-bound predicate only when the lower endpoint meets its threshold. Pass an upper-bound predicate only when the upper endpoint meets it, preserving the explicitly strict `<0.50` condition. Boundaries not decisively on either side are INCONCLUSIVE. These new inequalities do not replace the original C2 point-estimate reader or change the legacy Vow-5 predicate `vow==5 and best>0.90+1e-12`.
