01 / Architecture & scale

A city with depth

The library is the principal landmark. Smaller civic groups combine towers, reading wings and low flooded courts. Four submerged quarters suggest the wider city beneath the water. Each continuous bridge edge is divided into complete bays with two broad pointed openings. Capped posts mark the divisions. Inclines use upright stone balusters beneath a continuous sloping handrail, following the selected study. Short returns remain solid so bays do not end in clipped half-openings.

Fitted stonework

Stairs, piers and continuous edges

Sustained steep inclines are fitted as complete flights before the deck is built. Their endpoints retain the original arrival heights; equal-height horizontal treads follow the fitted incline, with crisp risers. Gentle slopes retain paving and junction platforms stay clear. The current sample contains 22 flights; the tallest riser is about 17 cm.

02 / Water & materials

The flood belongs to the buildings

Broken, elongated crests drift across layered teal water. The treatment is deliberately stylised, with a quieter palette for the drowned city. Crests soften as the camera pulls back, keeping the wider route plan readable. Shallow stone remains visible through restrained refraction, with broken foam at the waterline. Lower courts meet the same water level as the surrounding city, while the causeways stay dry. Large stone flags keep the ground quiet.

Eight seconds captured from this native candidate. The shared water surface fills both the courtyard and the surrounding city. Its graphic crests, depth colour, flow and shore treatment can be tuned separately for each chapter.

Review focus

B has been selected as the parapet language. This review checks its application to real generated bends, crossings and stairs, at both close and ordinary viewing distances.

What was checked
  • Geometry probes confirm two real openings per test bay, solid sills and coping, and correct faces for both boundary directions.
  • All 65 locations and 76 generated connections remain present.
  • Stair geometry checks cover gentle slopes, uphill/downhill flights and a curved incline: horizontal hard-edged tops, equal risers and consistent tread depth after grading.
  • 28,315 samples across the rendered walking corridor found no missing deck surface; sampled bridge landings meet at the same height.
  • Rendered bridge-width samples: minimum measured headroom approximately 2.36 m across all three crossings.
  • 12,135 sampled body positions found no conservative architectural obstruction; a separate 20,225-sample check found no obstruction from the added rail and pier triangles.
  • Phone and tablet reference views: selection, pan, zoom and overview controls exercised using native input.

These are bounded checks for this native Step 3 study. Campaign integration, wider layout qualification and final production performance remain later work.
